Ice hockey is a fast-paced team sport played on ice, where two teams of six players (including a goaltender) compete to score goals by shooting a rubber puck into the opposing team's net. The sport originated in Canada, with the first organized indoor game played in Montreal in 1875, and it has since become one of the most popular sports in countries such as Canada, Finland, Latvia, the Czech Republic, and Slovakia.

Modern hockey evolved from earlier stick-and-ball games like shinty, bandy, and field hockey. Today, it is governed internationally by the International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF), while the National Hockey League (NHL) is considered the world's top professional league. Men's hockey has been part of the Olympics since 1924, and women's hockey was added in 1998.

A standard game consists of three 20-minute periods. Players are divided into forwards, defencemen, and a goaltender. Teams move the puck using sticks, passing and shooting while following rules such as offside and icing. Physical play, including body checking in many leagues, is allowed within specific limits. If the score is tied, overtime may be played, and in some competitions a shootout determines the winner. The Stanley Cup, first awarded in 1893, is the NHL's championship trophy and one of the most prestigious awards in sports.
